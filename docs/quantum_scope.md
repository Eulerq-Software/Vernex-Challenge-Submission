# Quantum formulation: current status and proposed direction

## Scope and scientific status

The **implemented classical LABS solvers evaluate the original autocorrelation objective directly** on binary phases (`Q=2`); they do not use a QUBO encoding. The independent Gurobi code (`solvers/gurobi/gurobi_exact_solver.py`) **does use auxiliary binary XOR variables** and linear XOR-consistency constraints, with a convex quadratic objective in those auxiliaries. This is a **constrained MIQP**, not an unconstrained QUBO. In the supplied Git bundle there is **no implemented QAOA, quantum-annealing, quantum hardware or SimCIM benchmark for LABS**.

## Mathematical formulations

For spins `x_i ∈ {−1,+1}`, define `C_k = Σ_i x_i x_{i+k}` and `E = Σ_k C_k²`. Expanding the squares yields generally **two- and four-body terms**, not automatically a quadratic Ising model.

An equivalent representation uses bits `b_i=(1−x_i)/2` and pair-XOR auxiliaries `z_{i,k}=b_i XOR b_{i+k}`, giving `C_k = (N−k)−2Σ_i z_{i,k}`. The Gurobi implementation enforces the XOR identities through **linear inequalities** while minimizing `Σ C_k²`; the feasible integer solutions agree with LABS.

To obtain an **unconstrained** QUBO for a QUBO-only solver or annealer, one would have to replace consistency constraints by appropriately weighted *quadratic penalty functions*, with a rigorous penalty scale. That construction is a proposed method, **not the model currently used in the Gurobi implementation**. Alternatively, a gate-model quantum algorithm can implement the original higher-order diagonal Hamiltonian directly, using only the original `N` spins but potentially more circuit depth.

## Proposed hybrid algorithm (not yet benchmarked)

1. Start from a good sequence generated by the available classical solvers.
2. Choose a restricted neighbourhood with `m << N` active bits, fixing the others.
3. Form the **exact restricted cost Hamiltonian**, maintaining all interactions among active bits induced by the fixed outside spins.
4. Explore selected neighbourhood states with a suitable quantum optimizer or sampler (for instance, QAOA on the higher-order Hamiltonian, or quantum annealing after valid quadratization).
5. Return measured or sampled states for direct full-objective evaluation, optionally followed by classical descent or a basin-escape test.
6. Compare wall-clock time and success probability against strong classical *neighbourhood-level* baselines at matched budgets, including all preparation, embedding and readout overhead.

**Caution:** Minimizing the energy of the restricted neighbourhood may not discover the productive *uphill triggers* seen in separate landscape experiments. A testable quantum research question is whether suitably chosen mixers, sampling distributions or surrogate trigger objectives improve the post-relaxation basin-escape rate. No advantage is asserted here.

## Extensions to `Q>2`

General `Q`-phase codes use `x_i=exp(−2πj q_i/Q)` with `q_i∈{0,...,Q−1}` and objective `Σ|ρ(k)|²`. Possible binary encodings include one-hot (`Q` bits/chip plus one-hot constraints) or compact binary (`ceil(log2(Q))` bits/chip plus admissibility restrictions). The resulting discrete nonlinear objective can be reduced to higher-order binary optimization and, with additional variables/penalties, to quadratic binary form. These encodings and specialized quantum solvers are future work, not benchmarked results.
